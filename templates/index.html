Methodology

1. Real-World Problem

Fixed solar panels installed at a suboptimal tilt/azimuth capture significantly less energy than optimally oriented panels. Given a location, date, and observation window, we want the panel orientation that maximizes captured solar energy.

2. Decision Variables

\( \beta \) — panel tilt angle from horizontal (degrees).
\( \gamma \) — panel azimuth angle, compass convention (0°=N, 90°=E, 180°=S, 270°=W).

3. Objective Function

Maximize total estimated energy collected over the time window \([t_0, t_1]\):

\[ E(\beta,\gamma) = A \cdot \eta \int_{t_0}^{t_1} G_{POA}(t;\beta,\gamma)\, dt \]

where \(A\) is panel area (m²), \(\eta\) is panel efficiency, and \(G_{POA}\) is the plane-of-array irradiance (W/m²):

\[ G_{POA} = \underbrace{G_{DNI}\cos\theta_i}_{\text{beam}} + \underbrace{G_{DHI}\frac{1+\cos\beta}{2}}_{\text{diffuse (isotropic sky)}} + \underbrace{G_{GHI}\,\rho\,\frac{1-\cos\beta}{2}}_{\text{ground-reflected}} \]

The angle of incidence \(\theta_i\) between the sun and the panel normal (Duffie & Beckman formulation):

\[ \cos\theta_i = \sin\alpha\cos\beta + \cos\alpha\sin\beta\cos(\gamma_s-\gamma) \]

with \(\alpha\)=solar altitude, \(\gamma_s\)=solar azimuth, \(\rho=0.2\) ground albedo. Negative \(\cos\theta_i\) (sun behind the panel) is clipped to zero.

4. Constraints

  • \(0^\circ \le \beta \le 90^\circ\) — physical tilt range
  • \(0^\circ \le \gamma < 360^\circ\) — full compass range
  • \(t \in [t_0, t_1]\) — user-selected observation window on a single day
  • \(G_{POA} \ge 0\) — irradiance cannot be negative; night-time contributes 0

5. Solar Position Calculation

Declination (Cooper, 1969): \(\delta = 23.45\sin\left(\frac{360}{365}(284+n)\right)\), where \(n\)=day of year.

Equation of time (minutes): \(EoT = 9.87\sin(2B) - 7.53\cos B - 1.5\sin B\), \(B=\frac{360}{365}(n-81)\).

Hour angle: \(H = 15(t_{solar}-12)\), where \(t_{solar}=t_{clock}+EoT/60\) (longitude/timezone correction is not modelled — documented assumption).

Altitude: \(\sin\alpha = \sin\phi\sin\delta + \cos\phi\cos\delta\cos H\).

Azimuth: \(\cos\gamma_s = \dfrac{\sin\delta - \sin\alpha\sin\phi}{\cos\alpha\cos\phi}\), reflected to \([0,360)\) using the sign of \(H\).

Clear-sky irradiance: beam irradiance uses a Meinel-type exponential air-mass attenuation \(G_{DNI}=I_0\cdot 0.7^{AM^{0.678}}\), \(AM=1/\sin\alpha\); diffuse is approximated as \(G_{DHI}=0.1\,G_{DNI}\); \(G_{GHI}=G_{DNI}\sin\alpha+G_{DHI}\). This is a standard simplified clear-sky approximation used in introductory solar engineering — it ignores clouds, aerosols, and local atmospheric variability.

6. Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)

Each particle \(i\) has a position \(x_i=(\beta_i,\gamma_i)\) and velocity \(v_i\) in the 2-D search space. At each iteration:

\[ v_i \leftarrow w\,v_i + c_1 r_1 (p_i - x_i) + c_2 r_2 (g - x_i) \]

\[ x_i \leftarrow x_i + v_i \]

where \(p_i\) is the particle's personal best, \(g\) is the swarm's global best, \(r_1,r_2\sim U(0,1)\), and \(w\) linearly decreases from \(w_{max}\) to \(w_{min}\) across iterations (favouring exploration early, exploitation later). Velocities and positions are clamped to stay within the physical bounds. The fitness of a particle is simply \(E(\beta_i,\gamma_i)\) — maximized directly (no penalty terms needed since bounds are enforced by clamping).

7. Baseline Methods

Grid Search (validation baseline): an exhaustive scan of the (tilt, azimuth) space at 2°×4° resolution (≈4,140 evaluations), used to (a) verify PSO converges to the same region of the search space and (b) render the full energy landscape.

Fixed/Default Orientation (naive real-world baseline): tilt = |latitude|, azimuth = 180° (south-facing, Northern Hemisphere) or 0° (north-facing, Southern Hemisphere) — a common rule-of-thumb used in practice without optimization.

8. Convergence & Performance Analysis

The convergence graph plots the best-so-far energy value after each PSO iteration, which is monotonically non-decreasing by construction. Good convergence behaviour shows rapid improvement in early iterations followed by a plateau near the global optimum. We additionally report the number of objective-function evaluations used by PSO vs. grid search: PSO typically reaches within a fraction of a percent of the exhaustive grid-search optimum using an order of magnitude fewer evaluations, demonstrating its efficiency as a metaheuristic for this continuous, low-dimensional, largely unimodal optimization landscape.
